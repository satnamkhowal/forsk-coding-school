Microsoft Security

AI brands are being used as social-engineering bait

Microsoft reports campaigns impersonating familiar AI tools in phishing, malvertising and fake software downloads.
02
Lesson #1

Verify the source before installing

Use official vendor sites or verified repositories. Do not treat a search result, ad or familiar logo as proof of authenticity.
03
Lesson #2

Treat urgency as a warning signal

Account suspension, payment deadlines and “install now” prompts should trigger independent verification, not faster clicking.
04
Lesson #3

Understand identity flows

Know which account is being connected, what permissions are requested and why a device-code or OAuth approval is needed.
05
Lesson #4

Visual trust is not software trust

A polished README, star count or professional installer can be copied. Check provenance, publisher and release history.
06
Lesson #5

Connect events across the attack path

Email, redirects, downloads and unusual sign-ins may belong to one incident. Reconstruct the sequence instead of viewing each signal alone.
